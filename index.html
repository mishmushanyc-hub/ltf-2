Vol. 04 is an evening in conversation with stylist Heidi Cannon — an intimate dinner party in Williamsburg shaped by her perspective on food, memory, and the lives we carry with us.

Guest Profile — Heidi Cannon

Heidi grew up everywhere and nowhere at the same time — moving between Hawaii, Florida, California, West Virginia, Philly, and eventually New York. Home was never a place, it was something more fluid. Music, memory, and small sensory moments stuck more than geography ever did.

Her relationship to food mirrors that. It swings between extremes — Spam and Spaghettios, sushi at six years old, diner breakfasts, anchovies on toast, caviar when she can get it. There's no strict logic, just instinct. A “freak palette,” as she puts it — where high and low, comfort and curiosity all sit at the same table. Each dish reflects a moment, a habit, or a phase, pulled from a life that's been constantly in motion but always grounded in feeling.

The Menu
Course 01

Goat Cheese & Pistachio Stuffed Figs

lil spicy, lil sweet

Figs come from an early fixation — Heidi references the Sylvia Plath “fig tree,” the idea of too many lives and not knowing which one to choose. This dish sets that tone: soft, sweet, a little sharp. It’s the starting point of everything — possibility, tension, and a slightly romanticized way of looking at the world.

Course 02

Charred Balsamic Brussels, Confit Garlic Purée

heaps and loads of parm on top

Heidi is a self-proclaimed “greens girl,” but not in a delicate way. She wants depth — char, sweetness, heaviness. This dish leans into that.

Course 03

Spam Onigiri

touch of wasabi for your nostrils

This is childhood, directly. Spam was always around, and sushi came early — introduced by parents who loved Japanese food. This dish sits right between those two worlds: something humble, something more refined, meeting in the middle. It’s also a reminder that nostalgia doesn’t have to be polished to be meaningful.

Course 04

Philly Cheesesteak Crêpe

truffle wiz for good measure

At 10 years old, Heidi spent an entire summer making thousands of crêpes, convinced she\'d become a French pastry chef. Years later, she lived in Philly. This dish connects those two versions of her — obsessive creativity and lived experience. Intentionally a little absurd. That balance between serious and unserious is very much her.

Dessert

Surprise

think: it’s Micho’s birthday

Vol. 05 is an evening with Ekaterina Semenova Conway — a painter and tattoo artist from Moscow, three years into New York. An intimate dinner in the East Village shaped by where she came from and everything she’s picked up since.

Guest Profile — Ekaterina Semenova Conway

Katya (Ekaterina Semenova Conway) is a Moscow-born tattoo artist and painter whose work lives in tension — between softness and aggression, control and chaos, intimacy and rebellion. Raised in a large family, full of intellectuals and shaped by early influences like chess and generational artistry, she developed a visual language that blends abstract realism with surreal, rule-breaking instinct.

Her journey from Russia to New York — through Paris and Barcelona — wasn’t just geographic, but deeply personal. Political activism, displacement, and lived resistance inform her perspective, giving her work an emotional weight that feels both confrontational and vulnerable. She creates to provoke — to make people uncomfortable, then draw them in closer.

Now based in New York, Katya channels that same philosophy into tattooing — treating the human body as both canvas and connection point. Her work is permanent, intimate, and unapologetically hers — marked by contrast, memory, and a refusal to follow rules.

The Menu
Course 01

Charcuterie — Parm, Jamón, Brie, Salted Fish, Black Rye

This mirrors how she eats at home — communal, unstructured, pulling from both European staples and Russian essentials. The black rye and salted fish ground this course in her upbringing, while the cheese and jamón reflect her life now.

Course 02

Patatas Bravas, Sriracha Aïoli

A nod to her time living in Spain, love for sriracha, and casual, shareable plates. It represents that late-night, slightly chaotic energy she thrives in.

Course 03

Rocket Salad — Roasted Peppers, Chestnut, Cherry Vinaigrette

Built from memory — arugula (rocket) from her grandmother’s garden, peppers she learned to love later, and chestnut and cherry bringing in Eastern European warmth and balance.

Course 04

Pelmeni — Russian Tom Yum Soup Dumplings

A fusion of her cultural base and her mom’s influence cooking Thai food at home. It represents movement, travel, and flavors colliding naturally.

Course 05

Fried Shrimp, Caviar, Crème Fraîche

Directly tied to her childhood — fried shrimp was a staple — but elevated with caviar to reflect her Russian roots and that contrast between grit and luxury.

To Close

Masala Chai & Russian Tea Cookies

Tea is ritual for her — something grounding at the end. The pairing keeps dessert light, nostalgic, and communal rather than overly indulgent.

Vol. 06 is an afternoon outside in Red Hook — two close friends, two different ways of life, one table. Doors at 5pm. Address shared one week prior.

Guest Profile — Miles Dixon & Elijuh Vuhlet

Elijah learned the value of family and community early. Growing up Dominican and Puerto Rican between Long Island and the LES, his first references for food and clothing were rooted in function. Over time that foundation evolved — he found punk, and with it a natural overlap between distressed aesthetics and workwear sensibility. The result feels honest. His clothes, his attitude, his presence all bring people together — don’t let the ripped hats or head tat confuse you.

Miles is a Brooklyn kid. He grew up on a MetroCard and a sense of freedom, moving through the city with curiosity. That world expanded when he moved to Germany, but eventually it pushed beyond place altogether. Miles leans toward the fantastical. Through his brand, Real Fantasy, he brings those ideas back down to earth — grounded in American workwear. His taste in food reflects that same utility, especially in his deep love for sandwiches. Whether it’s early memories at The Odeon or a lifelong connection to the BEC, everything he creates builds on the culture that raised him.

The Menu
Course 01

Beef Bacon Egg n Cheese

Yo Ock, get me a BEC on a roll

Smokey beef bacon, fluffy eggs, American cheese — there are no other options because it’s perfect in every way — and spicy ketchup.

Vegetarian Egg Sando — spicy ketchup & hash browns

Our conversation with Miles made two things clear. He is NY through and through and is passionate about his sandwiches. No better way to honor this than going right for the NY sandwich jugular.

Course 02

Wedge Salad

Brasserie food meets the Caribbean. What’s more New York?

Chicharrón de pollo, fried cheese, pickled red onions, passionfruit ranch.

Vegetarian Wedge Salad — fried cheese, pickled onions & passionfruit ranch

At the intersection of where things begin and where they are now. It was Miles who birthed the idea of expanding this event to include Elijah. A humble act of spotlight sharing that inspired this course. Miles’ childhood memories at The Odeon paired seamlessly with Elijah’s Caribbean roots. This dish is what LTF is all about.

Course 03

Whole Roasted Hog

An LTF twist on a Dominican classic

Served w/ Moro de Habichuelas and tostones. Crispy skin, fragrant rice con caperberries y frijoles, and pull-apart pork.

Vegetarian Smoked Cauliflower Steak — Moro de Habichuelas & tostones

Y’all, it’s a cookout. Spending time with Elijah made one thing obvious: everything comes back to community. This had to be big, shared, and felt. A proper finale rooted in gathering and kinship.

To Close

Compressed Watermelon Poppers

The brainchild of guest chef Ayele Mystic

Lemon, shiso, West Indian bay leaf. Served cold — engineered to refresh.

Sitting 01 centers on Nicole Ko, the floral artist behind Ephemera. Her practice is built on impermanence: arrangements made to be experienced once, then released. 13 Grattan St, Suite 406, Brooklyn.

Subject Notes ▼

Nicole Ko is a floral artist and the founder of Ephemera, a wedding floral design studio established in 2019. Nicole has been working with the floral medium since 2012, carefully refining a craft that lives within the intersection of fine art and living material.

Nicole grew up in the rural South. A place where nature was simply the world around her. Her move to NY only deepened that relationship. She was drawn to the botanic gardens, her eye fixed on the transitions of each season.

Ephemera, meaning transient, fleeting, and beautiful. Here one minute, gone the next. Today it is her life's work and she approaches every wedding with this sacred mindset. The flowers will not last, but the memories they enhance surely will.

Her work is known for its expressive use of line, its intuitive relationship with color, and an approach to floral design that feels less like arrangement and more like composition. She believes beauty is not a luxury but a need. Something essential. To her, beauty is food for the soul.

Findings (Menu) ▼
Course 01

Fried Green Tomatoes w/ Smoked Herring and Pumpkin Crema

Nicole's story began on the creekbeds of Georgia developing a deeply personal relationship with the world around her. The first course of this menu is an homage to this chapter of her life. Fried Green Tomatoes serve as a touchpoint for her early memories in Georgia. At 10 she was first exposed to Kiki's Delivery Service. Hayao Miyazaki's feminine protagonist pushed her to nurture her own creative ecosystem and continues to remind her to enjoy life and leave space for new creative ideas. In reference to this foundational inspiration, her fried green tomatoes are topped with a smoked herring and pumpkin crema, our take on Kiki's most difficult delivery.

Course 02

Dumplings 2 Ways

Chicken and Dumpling Gazpacho w/ Citrus Poulet Composé ┃ Fried Dumplings w/ Baby Corn and Chrysanthemum Leaves

When Nicole needs to think of family she makes Chicken and Dumplings. She was raised in a military family which meant constantly moving around. Our second course plays on this constant change, taking what is usually hot and making it cold, what is usually straightforward and creating something bold.

Chinese food can be found everywhere, but like so many cuisines it feels almost inherent to NY. There is no understanding Nicole's art without first understanding how NY has shaped her. A painter turned florist. NY carves a path for all those who call her home and our fried dumpling course pays tribute to this truth.

Course 03

Pasta al Nasturtium w/ Brown Butter, Morel Mushroom, and Green Apple

Ephemera means something that lasts only a short while. Flowers, pasta sauce emulsions, an event of this very nature. With Nicole, there is so much more than what meets the eye. Like a flower we only see what has budded thru the surface. Our final savory course tells this very story. A sauce simple in color, bursting with flavor. Eat it while it's hot, its truest form is fast fleeting.

Dessert

Chocolate Fire and Ice Cream

Our dessert is a glimpse into the future for Nicole. Her dream is to create a book that highlights not only her own work but the spectrum of emotions that flowers convey. Her art is intrinsically focused on pulling people into the present moment. Stunning arrangements that grant people permission to enjoy themselves and allow the beauty of the world to penetrate deep. Our dessert marries her love of chocolate with this momentous artistic responsibility. A play on all the senses, a grand finale for a subject whose work is so often the explosion of color that makes memories last forever.

About Life Thru Food
LTF  ·  Archive  ·  2024–Present
About

Life Thru Food is a monthly dinner series in New York City. Each Sitting is built around a Subject: someone whose background, culture, and creative life shapes every course.

Food is not the subject of the story. It is how we communicate it.

The Sitting

A Sitting begins before anyone sits down. Before each dinner, LTF spends time with the Subject in their own space, listening. From that conversation, a menu is written from scratch: every recipe original, every course a direct response to what we heard.

The dinner itself is the translation. Hospes arrive to a table shaped by one person's roots, culture, and way of moving through the world, expressed through food they have never tasted before and will never taste the same way again.

Over a year, twelve Sittings become a running record: twelve subjects, twelve original menus, one table.

The Session

Sessions are smaller, family-style dinners shared with our community. Where a Sitting is built around getting to know one Subject, a Session is built around getting to know our guests. Each one is invite only.
